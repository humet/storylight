# Story Series

## Purpose

A Storylight series is a complete planned narrative delivered as nightly chapters.

## Series creation

Before Chapter 1:

1. Validate idea and parent preferences.
2. Build fixed Story DNA.
3. Generate the complete Series Bible.
4. Run structural and semantic validation.
5. Persist the accepted bible.
6. Pin model, prompt, schema, and visual profiles.
7. Generate Chapter 1.

## Series Bible contents

- title
- spoiler-free premise
- internal synopsis
- chapter count
- emotional promise
- world rules
- cast
- central question
- central conflict
- planned ending
- character arcs
- plot threads
- chapter blueprints
- immutable facts
- forbidden developments

## Chapter blueprint

Each chapter blueprint defines:

- narrative purpose
- opening state
- local goal
- conflict
- major beats
- emotional movement
- information revealed
- threads introduced, advanced, or resolved
- closing state
- gentle tomorrow promise

Blueprints describe what happens, not the final prose.

## Chapter shape

A nightly chapter should normally include:

1. Brief reorientation without repetitive recap.
2. A clear local goal.
3. Two to five meaningful scenes.
4. A discovery, choice, obstacle, or relationship movement.
5. A local emotional resolution.
6. Gentle anticipation for tomorrow.

## Chapter count

MVP options:

- 5 chapters
- 10 chapters

An advanced custom count may support 3–20 after validation. Shorter series reduce continuity and illustration cost and should be the default.

## Pacing

A ten-chapter baseline might use:

1. Invitation or disruption
2. Entering the new situation
3. Early progress
4. Complication
5. Important discovery
6. Setback or disagreement
7. Consequences
8. Repair and new plan
9. Climax
10. Resolution and calm return

This is guidance, not a mandatory formula.

## Spoilers

The hidden bible and future blueprints must never appear in child-facing APIs. Parent-facing series settings may show chapter count and spoiler-free premise only.

## Next chapter generation

The next chapter workflow loads:

- pinned series bible
- exact chapter blueprint
- latest accepted continuity snapshot
- relevant character profiles
- current visual profile
- parent preferences

Only one workflow may generate a chapter number.

## Completion

A series is complete when:

- final chapter is published
- central conflict is resolved
- central threads are resolved
- character arcs reach their final states
- continuity records the settled end state
- the ending becomes calm and emotionally reassuring

## Regeneration

Before later chapters exist, a chapter may be regenerated with a new continuity chain.

After later chapters exist, regeneration is constrained to preserve dependent outcomes.

## Acceptance criteria

- Chapter 1 is never written without an accepted bible.
- Every chapter has a distinct purpose.
- Central threads resolve at the planned time.
- The final chapter pays off earlier setup.
- The complete series feels authored rather than episodic.
