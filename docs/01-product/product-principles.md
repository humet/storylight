# Storylight Product Principles

## 1. The bedtime experience comes first

Storylight should support a calm shared ritual. It must not use addictive engagement patterns, infinite feeds, loud notifications, or pressure to continue reading.

## 2. Storytelling before technology

The product speaks about stories, chapters, books, characters, and illustrations. It does not expose prompts, tokens, model names, or internal workflow stages to children.

## 3. Character consistency is sacred

A recurring child-character must remain recognisable in appearance, personality, relationships, and history. Identity is enforced through structured profiles, approved references, and review.

## 4. Every series is planned before it begins

A full hidden series bible exists before Chapter 1. This enables pacing, foreshadowing, meaningful character arcs, and a proper ending.

## 5. Continuity is data

The system stores structured state for possessions, knowledge, location, relationships, plot threads, outfits, and world conditions. It does not rely on conversational memory.

## 6. Every chapter has a purpose

A chapter must change the story, reveal something, deepen a relationship, develop a character, or resolve a thread.

## 7. Every bedtime session ends well

A wider mystery may remain open, but the local emotional beat should feel complete. Avoid ending at maximum danger.

## 8. Mobile is the default

Every flow must work well in portrait orientation, in low light, with one hand, while a parent may be holding a child.

## 9. Less interface, more story

During reading, the interface should disappear. Controls must be minimal, predictable, and secondary to the book.

## 10. Parents stay in control

Parents can set boundaries, choose length, regenerate content, report problems, and manage character profiles.

## 11. Children create; they do not configure

Children should be able to say “an axolotl pirate on the moon.” They should not need to understand genre, narrative structure, or model settings.

## 12. Illustrations are narrative assets

Images should establish mood, reveal detail, and support the text. They are not decorative filler.

## 13. Premium over quantity

One story worth rereading is more valuable than ten forgettable generations.

## 14. Deterministic where correctness matters

Use code for workflow, identity, continuity, validation, publication, and permissions. Use models for creative language, planning proposals, and image generation.

## 15. Trust is earned slowly

A single frightening image, broken character identity, or contradictory chapter can damage confidence. Release gates must reflect that.

## Decision checklist

Before implementing a feature:

1. Does it improve the bedtime experience?
2. Does it work beautifully on a phone?
3. Does it reduce effort for parents?
4. Does it preserve identity and continuity?
5. Does it avoid overstimulation?
6. Can it be maintained for years?
7. Can its failure mode be made safe?
